TECHNOLOGY

The fundamental operating principle of Spectril devices is Raman spectrography. This non-destructive technique analyzes the inelastic scattering of monochromatic laser light interacting with molecular vibrations within a sample. Because rotational-vibrational molecular structures provide a unique "fingerprint" for each molecule, Raman spectra allow for the unambiguous identification and quantitative measurement of compounds.

Spectril devices overcome the inherent challenges of field spectroscopy through sophisticated optical engineering and intelligent sensor integration. At the core of each system, holographic filters suppress the intense Rayleigh scattering that occurs at the laser excitation line, allowing for clear detection of the Stokes and anti-Stokes Raman signals that carry molecular identification information

The environmental sensor suite integrated into Spectril devices extends far beyond basic spectroscopic measurement, transforming each unit into a comprehensive water quality monitoring platform. Parameters such as conductivity, dissolved oxygen, pH, and total dissolved solids are measured concurrently with spectral data, providing the contextual information essential for accurate pollutant quantification in natural waters. Conductivity measurements prove particularly valuable as they correlate strongly with nitrate concentration variability, often indicating ionic contributions from agricultural runoff or industrial discharge. Turbidity sensing plays a critical role in optical interference correction, allowing the system to compensate for light scattering effects that would otherwise degrade spectral quality in sediment-laden or algae-rich samples.

Every Spectril device is built around a Raspberry Pi embedded system, chosen specifically for its minimal power consumption, compact form factor, and extensive community support for field instrumentation applications. This processing architecture enables sophisticated onboard data analysis, real-time spectral visualization, and automated calibration routines while maintaining the portability essential for remote deployment. Standard GPS integration across all models ensures that every measurement is geographically referenced, creating spatially aware datasets that can be mapped and analyzed to reveal pollution patterns, identify contamination sources, and track water quality trends over time. The combination of embedded processing, comprehensive sensing, and location awareness makes Spectril devices uniquely suited for both stationary monitoring stations and mobile survey applications.

DATA

Spectral Baseline Correction: Accurate analysis in Raman spectroscopy requires robust baseline correction to isolate the weak molecular signals from strong background interference, often caused by fluorescence. Spectril systems implement automated calibration routines that utilize techniques designed for optimal performance, such as algorithms based on penalized least squares (e.g., AirPLS) or newer hybrid deep learning methods. These advanced methods significantly enhance the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) by removing polynomial and exponential backgrounds derived from fluorescence or Rayleigh scattering, often achieving tenfold improvements over raw spectra


Spectral Preprocessing and Data Quality

The foundation of Spectril's analytical capabilities rests on rigorous spectral preprocessing that transforms raw Raman measurements into chemically meaningful signals. Automated baseline correction algorithms, particularly those based on penalized least squares such as Asymmetrically Reweighted Penalized Least Squares (AirPLS), systematically remove the polynomial and exponential backgrounds that arise from fluorescence and residual Rayleigh scattering. These methods adaptively fit the baseline while preserving the integrity of genuine Raman peaks, often achieving signal-to-noise ratio improvements exceeding tenfold compared to uncorrected spectra. More recent hybrid approaches incorporating deep learning architectures further enhance correction performance in challenging field conditions where fluorescence interference varies unpredictably. This preprocessing pipeline is complemented by comprehensive data hygiene protocols including outlier detection using statistical distance metrics, intelligent missing value imputation based on spectral similarity, and normalization schemes that account for variations in laser power and integration time across measurement sessions.


Chemometric Analysis and Quantification

For quantitative pollutant analysis, Spectril employs chemometric techniques that exploit the fundamental relationship between Raman intensity and molecular concentration. The system maintains an extensive reference spectral library, enabling the construction of calibration models through multivariate regression methods. Partial Least Squares (PLS) regression proves particularly valuable for complex environmental samples where spectral overlap between chemical species creates analytical challenges that univariate approaches cannot resolve. PLS decomposes the spectral data into latent variables that capture the maximum covariance between the measured spectra and target analyte concentrations, enabling simultaneous quantification of multiple species even in matrices as challenging as estuarine waters with variable salinity.


Key capabilities include:

  • Detection limits suitable for regulatory compliance monitoring across environmentally relevant concentration ranges
  • Simultaneous quantification of multiple chemical species (nitrates, nitrites, phosphates) from a single spectrum
  • Matrix effect compensation through integration of auxiliary sensor data (conductivity, pH, temperature, dissolved oxygen)


Advanced Machine Learning Applications

When addressing highly nonlinear environmental relationships, Spectril extends beyond traditional chemometric methods to leverage advanced machine learning architectures tailored to specific analytical challenges:

  • Microplastic Identification: One-dimensional convolutional neural networks process raw Raman spectra to automatically extract discriminative spectral features characteristic of different polymer types, eliminating the need for manual peak assignment and achieving classification accuracies exceeding traditional methods like Support Vector Machines. The Spectril Pro model's integrated microscopy capability enables complementary image-based classification using two-dimensional CNNs trained to recognize microplastic particles by morphological characteristics, creating a multimodal identification system that cross-validates spectroscopic and visual evidence.
  • Predictive Water Quality Modeling: For predicting complex water quality dynamics such as seasonal nitrate fluctuations in agricultural watersheds, Generalized Additive Mixed Models (GAMMs) combine the multi-sensor environmental data streams with temporal and spatial covariates, capturing nonlinear relationships through flexible spline-based smoothing functions while accounting for hierarchical data structures arising from repeated measurements at monitoring sites. This sophisticated modeling framework transforms the Spectril platform from a simple analytical instrument into an intelligent environmental monitoring system capable of both immediate pollutant identification and predictive water quality forecasting.
