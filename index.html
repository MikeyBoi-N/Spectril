Nitrate

Nitrate is hard to measure outside a lab

Nitrate gets into water from fertilizer, manure and septic systems. It has no colour, taste or smell. EPA limits it to 10 mg/L as nitrogen in drinking water because high levels are dangerous for infants. Getting a reliable number in the field is difficult for three reasons.

  • It absorbs deep-UV lightUV methods measure how much light nitrate absorbs near 220–240 nm. Ordinary glass and most plastics block those wavelengths.
  • Other things absorb the same lightNitrite, dissolved organic matter and particles also absorb or scatter in that range, so a simple reading overstates nitrate.
  • Small drifts matterAt 235 nm the signal changes by about 20% for each nanometre the light source drifts, so temperature and LED ageing have to be tracked.
How Spectril deals with this

Why it matters

Most testing happens in labs

Public water systems test on a schedule set by law. Private wells, streams and farm ponds depend on someone taking a sample to a lab and waiting for the result.

Hardware

How a test works

You filter a sample into a small quartz cell. Two UV LEDs shine through the lower half of the cell, and a photodiode measures how much light gets through. If the Raman module is fitted, a 785 nm laser passes through the upper half and a camera records the light it scatters.

  1. FilterA 0.45 µm syringe filter removes particles that would scatter light.
  2. UV235 nm measures nitrate. 275 nm measures organic matter so it can be corrected for.
  3. RamanAdds sulfate, and checks that a high UV reading really is nitrate.
  4. RecordRaw readings, calibration, position and time are saved with every result.

Accuracy

How results are checked

Low-cost sensors can drift without anyone noticing. Spectril runs a set of checks during every test, and it reports a range with a verdict instead of a bare number.

  1. Blank first. Each test starts with deionized water in the same cell.
  2. Reference diode. A second photodiode watches the LEDs directly, so changes in LED brightness cancel out.
  3. Dark readings. Readings with the LEDs off remove room light and amplifier offset.
  4. Temperature. The sample temperature is used to correct the result.
  5. Repeat reading. The 235 nm reading is taken again at the end. If it has moved, the test is rejected.
  6. Check standard. A 10 mg/L nitrate standard is run regularly to confirm the unit still reads correctly.

Design targets: within ±10% at 1 mg/L as nitrogen and above, a detection limit of 1 mg/L, and no sample over the limit reported as clear. Full specifications

Compared with other options

What's different about it

Nothing is added to the sample, and every result keeps the raw readings behind it, so anyone can check the work. A certified lab is still the only way to get a compliance result. Spectril helps decide which samples need one.
