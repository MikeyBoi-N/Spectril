Technical details

For scientists and auditors

Spectril is at the design stage. Every figure on this page is a design value, an estimate or a published figure from someone else's work. None has been measured on this device yet, and estimates are marked as such.

Processing

Each wavelength is read as a ratio of sample diode to reference diode, after subtracting dark readings, and compared with a deionized water blank:

A = −log₁₀[ ((S − S_dark) / (R − R_dark)) / ((S₀ − S₀_dark) / (R₀ − R₀_dark)) ]

S and R are the sample and reference diode readings, and the 0 subscript marks the blank. Nitrate is then estimated from A₂₃₅ and A₂₇₅ with temperature correction, using a model trained on samples with paired lab results, such as partial least squares.

  1. Reject the test if the repeat 235 nm reading differs from the first.
  2. Flag samples outside the range the model was trained on, instead of guessing.
  3. Report a range and a verdict, with nitrite included and disclosed.
  4. Transfer calibration between units with a simple correction, tested at Gate 4.

Why 785 nm over 638 nm: less fluorescence from organic matter, no overlap with chlorophyll emission near 685 nm, and less risk of glow from the quartz. The cost is a weaker signal, about 2.3× from wavelength alone, and probably lower camera sensitivity.

Known limitations

  • Screening only. US compliance analyses must be done by certified labs using listed methods, and UV absorbance (Standard Methods 4500-NO₃ B) isn't on the nitrate list.
  • Nitrite absorbs in the same region and is included in the UV result.
  • The two-wavelength organic correction is meant for low-organic water. A New Hampshire stream study found dissolved organics affected even a full-spectrum sensor.
  • Iodide and bisulfide interfere, per YSI. Brackish water and seawater are out of scope because of bromide.
  • Two or three wavelengths can't separate five unknowns. Filtering and limited scope have to cover the rest.
  • The Raman channel's reach on a Pi camera is unproven. With about 90 mW it may only see high concentrations.
  • The 220 nm LEDs some listings advertise weren't credible: one "222 nm" part specifies a 230–238 nm peak in its own table.
